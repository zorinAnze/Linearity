Linear Regression

Linear regression attempts to model the relationship between two variables by fitting a linear equation to observed data. A linear regression line has an equation of the form Y = a + bX, where X is the explanatory variable and Y is the dependent variable. The most common method for fitting a regression line is the method of least-squares.


Homoscedasticity Assessment

Homoscedasticity, or homogeneity of variances, is an assumption of equal or similar variances in different groups being compared e.g. mesured samples at different concentration levels. In the app, three different tests, namely Cochran's test, Levene's test, and Fligner-Killeen's test, are used to test it.
If Cochran's variance outlier test produces a significant p-value while the other two tests do not, it is important to consider the Q-Q plot of the residuals. If a visual examination of the residual Q-Q plot reveals that the residuals are mostly normally distributed, with only a few values slightly deviating from the normal distribution but not to a concerning extent, the results can be considered acceptable. Cochran's variance outlier test is highly sensitive to outliers and non-normality, even when marginal1. This sensitivity could account for the significant p-value and the negative test outcome. In such situations, Levene's test may be more appropriate as it is more robust2 to deviations from normality.

1 Wang, Yan et al. “Comparing the Performance of Approaches for Testing the Homogeneity of Variance Assumption in One-Factor ANOVA Models.” Educational and psychological measurement vol. 77,2 (2017): 305-329
2 National Institute of Standards and Technology. Engineering Statistics Handbook. Section 1.3.5.10
